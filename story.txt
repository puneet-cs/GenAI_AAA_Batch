THE MONKEY WHO WANTED TO BECOME A SCIENTIST

Once upon a time, deep inside a colorful jungle, there lived a little monkey named Momo.

Momo was different from the other monkeys.

While everyone else loved climbing trees, eating bananas, and playing all day, Momo loved asking questions.

“Why does rain fall down?”

“Why do bananas turn yellow?”

“Can I make a banana fly?”

The other monkeys laughed.

One monkey said, “Momo, you are a monkey! Monkeys eat bananas. They don't do science!”

Momo adjusted his tiny pair of glasses and said,

“Why not?”

So Momo decided to become a scientist.

He built a laboratory inside an old tree.

On the door, he proudly wrote:

MOMO'S GREAT SCIENCE LAB
PLEASE DO NOT TOUCH ANYTHING!

Unfortunately, Momo himself touched everything.

His first experiment was called:

THE FLYING BANANA MACHINE.

Momo put a banana inside a small box.

He connected the box to some sticks, leaves, and a very old bicycle wheel.

“Three... two... one... LAUNCH!”

WHOOSH!

The banana flew through the air.

Momo jumped with excitement.

“I DID IT!”

But the banana did not land on the table.

It landed on Momo's head.

PLOP!

Momo looked up.

“Hmm... My experiment worked perfectly.”

A parrot nearby asked, “What was the purpose?”

Momo thought for a second.

“To make myself hungry.”

Everyone laughed.

Momo started another experiment.

This time he wanted to make a super-fast banana-peeling machine.

He placed a banana inside his machine.

“Let's test it!”

The machine started spinning.

WHIRRRR!

The banana disappeared.

Momo looked inside.

Nothing.

“Where did the banana go?”

Suddenly...

PLOP!

A banana peel landed on his head.

Then another.

PLOP!

And another.

PLOP!

Soon Momo was wearing five banana peels like a hat.

The parrot shouted,

“Congratulations, Professor Momo! You invented the world's first banana-peel hat!”

Momo laughed.

But he didn't give up.

One day, Momo noticed that the jungle animals were having trouble carrying heavy fruits.

“I will invent a machine that helps everyone carry things,” he announced.

He collected sticks, vines, wheels, and coconut shells.

After many attempts, he built a strange-looking machine.

It had three wheels, two handles, and one enormous coconut on top.

Momo pushed a button.

BEEP!

The machine moved forward.

Slowly at first...

Then faster...

Then VERY FAST!

“STOP!” shouted Momo.

But the machine didn't stop.

It raced through the jungle.

The rabbits jumped out of the way.

The squirrels climbed trees.

The elephants shouted, “MOVE!”

Momo chased after it.

“WAIT FOR ME! I'M THE SCIENTIST!”

Finally, the machine crashed into a giant pile of soft leaves.

FWOOF!

Leaves flew everywhere.

Momo climbed out.

The jungle became silent.

Then everyone started laughing.

Momo looked at his broken machine.

For a moment, he felt sad.

Professor Owl came over and said,

“Don't worry, Momo. Scientists don't succeed every time.”

Momo looked surprised.

“They don't?”

“No,” said Professor Owl. “Scientists experiment, make mistakes, learn from them, and try again.”

Momo smiled.

“So my broken machine is not a failure?”

“Exactly,” said Professor Owl. “It is an experiment that taught you something.”

Momo immediately grabbed his notebook.

He wrote:

LESSON 1:
DON'T BUILD A MACHINE WITH ONLY ONE BRAKE.

The animals laughed.

From that day on, Momo continued experimenting.

He invented a rain collector for the jungle.

He built a tiny solar cooker.

He even created a machine that counted bananas.

Although...

The banana counter sometimes counted Momo's tail as a banana.

“BANANAS: 7!”

Momo looked at the machine.

“I only have six bananas!”

The machine beeped.

“BANANAS: 7!”

Momo looked behind him.

His tail was sitting inside the machine.

“Oh!”

Everyone burst out laughing.

Years later, Momo became the most famous scientist in the jungle.

But he never forgot his first experiments.

Whenever a young monkey asked,

“Can I become a scientist?”

Momo smiled and said,

“Of course! Ask questions, try new things, make mistakes, learn, and never stop being curious.”

Then he would quietly add,

“And please... NEVER put your tail inside a banana counter.”

THE END
